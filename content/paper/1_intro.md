# Introduction

Solving heterogeneous-agent models with multiple simultaneous decisions has traditionally required either joint optimization, which is slow, or restrictive assumptions about preferences that limit economic relevance. These heterogeneous-agent models, in the tradition of {cite:t}`Huggett1993`, {cite:t}`Aiyagari1994`, and {cite:t}`KrusellSmith1998`, are the workhorses of macroeconomic analysis {cite:p}`kmpHandbook2016`, and increasingly they ask the household to make several decisions at once, such as labor supply, portfolio choice, or illiquid saving, the setting in which joint optimization grows costly. The Endogenous Grid Method (EGM) transformed computation of consumption-saving problems by inverting first-order conditions, but extending that insight to multiple continuous choices has proved difficult. The leading extensions either constrain the problem class or keep a numerical solve inside the iteration {cite:p}`Iskhakov2015,Druedahl2017,Druedahl2021`.

A household choosing consumption and labor supply makes both decisions with the same information. Yet the solver need not treat them jointly. I introduce Sequential EGM (EGM$^n$, whose superscript records the chain of stagewise inversions), which orders the problem into stages and applies an EGM inversion wherever the stage permits one. Stages without an inversion fall back to lower-dimensional optimization.

Each stage of an EGM$^n$ decomposition applies EGM when its first-order condition and its recovery map, the composite of the inverted condition with the budget identities, satisfy the inversion and optimality conditions developed in the paper. The recovery map carries each post-decision gridpoint to its endogenous pre-decision state, as $\mathfrak{m}(\aRat)=\aRat+\mathfrak{c}(\aRat)$ does in one-dimensional EGM, where $\aRat$ is end-of-period saving, $\mathfrak{c}$ the consumption the inverted Euler equation assigns to it, and $\mathfrak{m}$ the market resources from which that choice is optimal. Throughout, choices are continuous and satisfy standard constraint qualifications. EGM$^n$ requires stronger stage structure than nested approaches that embed optimization within the EGM iteration, but it avoids optimization at qualifying stages and parallelizes across grid cells because the closed-form inversions have no cross-cell dependencies.

On the two-asset model of {cite:t}`Auclert2021`, with both solvers in my own matched implementation, EGM$^n$ is faster than the Nested Endogenous Grid Method (NEGM) at every grid and thread count tested, by roughly an order of magnitude in serial and by about $3.7$ to $7.9$ times even at sixteen matched threads. Threads narrow the gap because the nested search has per-node work to spread across them, while the closed-form inversion has almost none. For the curvilinear grids the inversions produce, I develop ENGINE (ENdogenous Grid INterpolation and Extrapolation), with which the full solve runs roughly three to four and a half times faster than with the curvilinear interpolation previously proposed by {cite:t}`White2015` for such grids, at matched accuracy on the two-asset application.

As a direct product of the backward step, the decomposition also returns intermediate-stage marginal values, quantities single-block solvers never construct. In the two-asset model, their ratio minus one is the adjustment-cost wedge that prices converting illiquid wealth into liquid.

The Endogenous Grid Method was built to avoid the cost of searching over choices: for a three-choice problem with $G$ grid points per dimension, that search costs $O(G^3)$ evaluations at every state, a price paid across the whole state space.[^maliar-survey] {cite:t}`Carroll2006` introduces it for the consumption-saving problem: starting from a grid of post-decision states, EGM inverts the first-order condition to recover the optimal policy and the pre-decision state that implies it, so a numerical search becomes a function evaluation. {cite:t}`Barillas2007` extends it to a second control, the labor-leisure choice, in a neoclassical growth model.[^egm-extensions]

[^maliar-survey]: See {cite:t}`Maliar2014` for a thorough survey of numerical methods for such problems.

[^egm-extensions]: See also {cite:t}`Maliar2013`.

In the decade after EGM's introduction, its scope broadened along two fronts. On the structural side, {cite:t}`Fella2014` relaxes continuity requirements for non-convex problems and {cite:t}`Hintermaier2010` handles occasionally binding constraints among endogenous variables; {cite:t}`Druedahl2017` combines both advances into G2EGM, which admits multiple controls and states. On the discrete-choice side, {cite:t}`Iskhakov2017` incorporates jumps between alternatives through extreme value errors. Their method, however, remains restricted to a single control and state variable.[^egm-scope] Separately, {cite:t}`Clausen2020` develops Reverse Calculus for nested and non-convex optimization; G2EGM {cite:p}`Druedahl2017` builds its first-order-condition arguments on an earlier working-paper version of these differentiability results.

[^egm-scope]: On the preference side, {cite:t}`HallengreenJorgensenOlesen2024` develop iEGM for utility functions lacking a closed-form inverse marginal utility, and {cite:t}`Jorgensen2013` provides Monte Carlo evidence that EGM dominates value function iteration and MPEC in speed and robustness for structural estimation.

EGM$^n$ builds most directly on the multivariate extensions. {cite:t}`White2015` formalizes the conditions under which EGM applies to multidimensional problems and builds interpolation methods for the curvilinear grids that result; {cite:t}`Ludwig2018` proposes Delaunay triangulation for the same grids, though triangulation costs can offset the time saved by avoiding grid search. {cite:t}`Iskhakov2015` characterizes a triangular structure in the system of first-order conditions that permits sequential solution by substitution, with no root-finding.[^related-foc] Triangular EGM asks for triangularity of the entire system; EGM$^n$ asks only for sequential invertibility, stage by stage, which admits the mixed structures described above. {cite:t}`Druedahl2021` develops NEGM, which pairs an upper envelope with a one-dimensional numerical solve nested in the outer EGM iteration; nesting cuts complexity relative to joint optimization but keeps the embedded solve, which EGM$^n$ removes.[^high-dim]

[^related-foc]: A parallel line exploits first-order conditions without EGM's grid inversion: the envelope condition method of {cite:t}`Maliar2013` and the fixed-point iteration of {cite:t}`Mendoza2020` for models with occasionally binding constraints.

[^high-dim]: For state spaces beyond EGM's reach, global-solution methods approximate policies or equilibrium conditions with Gaussian processes or neural networks, trading interpretable policy functions for scale {cite:p}`Scheidegger2019,Maliar2021,Azinovic2022`; separately, {cite:t}`BayerLuettickeWeissWinkelmann2024` carry the endogenous-gridpoint idea to the law of motion of the wealth distribution.
