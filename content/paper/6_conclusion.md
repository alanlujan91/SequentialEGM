# Conclusion

We began by noting the tension between computational efficiency and economic realism in heterogeneous agent models. The Sequential Endogenous Grid Method resolves this tension through complementary contributions. On the conceptual side, we recognize that "simultaneous" from an economic perspective does not require "joint" from a computational perspective. Separable structure lets us decompose multidecision problems into sequential stages that each admit an EGM inversion. On the computational side, we develop interpolation methods that handle the non-rectilinear grids EGM generates, overcoming the bottleneck that has limited sequential approaches. Curvilinear Grid Interpolation exploits the preserved topology of curvilinear grids, while Gaussian Process Regression handles fully unstructured grids where even topological regularity breaks down. Together, these contributions achieve the efficiency of EGM methods even in settings with multiple decisions and high-dimensional state spaces.

The Sequential Endogenous Grid Method (EGM$^n$) introduced in this paper offers a novel approach to solving dynamic stochastic optimization problems with multiple decisions. The method separates problems into a sequence of smaller subproblems, each potentially admitting an EGM inversion, thereby chaining efficiency gains across decision stages. The interpolation challenge posed by resulting endogenous grids (whether curvilinear or fully unstructured) is addressed through methods tailored to the grid's geometric properties: Curvilinear Grid Interpolation for curvilinear grids and Gaussian Process Regression for unstructured grids.

EGM$^n$ is similar to the Nested Endogenous Grid Method (NEGM) of {cite:t}`Druedahl2021` and the Generalized Endogenous Grid Method (G2EGM) of {cite:t}`Druedahl2017` in that it can solve problems with multiple decisions, but differs by exploiting full separability to chain multiple EGM inversions without any root-finding or optimization steps. This requires stronger structural assumptions than the triangular FOC systems of {cite:t}`Iskhakov2015` or the envelope methods of G2EGM, but when separability holds, eliminates computational overhead entirely. However, unlike G2EGM, which handles discrete choices and non-convexities through upper envelope techniques, EGM$^n$ focuses on continuous choice problems where first-order conditions uniquely characterize optima. Extending EGM$^n$ to handle non-convexities and discrete choices remains an important direction for future research. The contribution here is demonstrating how appropriate interpolation methods (CGI for curvilinear grids and GPR for unstructured grids) overcome computational bottlenecks that have limited the application of sequential EGM approaches, particularly when compared to Delaunay triangulation methods.
