# Conclusion

Heterogeneous agent models face a tension between computational efficiency and economic realism. The Sequential Endogenous Grid Method resolves this tension through two contributions. Economically simultaneous decisions need not be computationally joint. When separable structure permits decomposition, each stage admits an EGM inversion, chaining efficiency gains across decisions. The non-rectilinear grids that EGM generates require specialized interpolation. Curvilinear Grid Interpolation exploits preserved topology in curvilinear grids. Gaussian Process Regression handles fully unstructured grids where topological regularity breaks down. These methods extend EGM efficiency to settings with multiple decisions and high-dimensional state spaces.

EGM$^n$ is similar to the Nested Endogenous Grid Method (NEGM) of {cite:t}`Druedahl2021` and the Generalized Endogenous Grid Method (G2EGM) of {cite:t}`Druedahl2017` in that it can solve problems with multiple decisions, but differs by exploiting full separability to chain multiple EGM inversions without any root-finding or optimization steps. This requires stronger structural assumptions than the triangular FOC systems of {cite:t}`Iskhakov2015` or the envelope methods of G2EGM, but when separability holds, eliminates computational overhead entirely. However, unlike G2EGM, which handles discrete choices and non-convexities through upper envelope techniques, EGM$^n$ focuses on continuous choice problems where first-order conditions uniquely characterize optima.

The contribution here is demonstrating how appropriate interpolation methods (CGI for curvilinear grids and GPR for unstructured grids) overcome computational bottlenecks that have limited the application of sequential EGM approaches, particularly when compared to Delaunay triangulation methods. By facilitating efficient solution methods, Sequential EGM could enable research on problems that have been computationally challenging: high-frequency portfolio rebalancing with transaction costs and multiple asset classes, multiperiod health insurance choices with endogenous health states and moral hazard, or durable goods decisions with both extensive and intensive margins. Extending EGM$^n$ to handle non-convexities, discrete choices, and exploring these applications remain important directions for future research.
